Guiding Question: What does this situation call for from me?

At the Meeting

  • Today's reading: Epictetus, Discourses 2.18.1–7. We'll read it aloud together.
  • The practice: Find the Appropriate Action in three steps.
    1. Notice. What facts, people, and responsibilities matter here?
    2. Choose. I will do this because it fits these circumstances.
    3. Follow through. When the cue appears, do it if it still fits.

Knowing What to Do—and Doing It

Someone is explaining a problem, and you're already preparing an answer. You interrupt. They stop. You meant to help, but you haven't yet heard what they needed to say.

March asks how appropriate action can become a steady practice. Begin with what the situation requires. Other people, your responsibilities, and what has changed all matter. Give a reason for a fitting response and then carry it out. If the same occasion recurs, a clear cue can help you remember the response before you fall into the old one.

Repeated choices help shape character. They also give us occasions to learn. A response that fits now may need adjustment when circumstances change. You can revise the plan, repair a missed opportunity where possible, and return at the next occasion.

The readings below connect reasons, responsibilities, and repetition. For ancient texts, use any translation you like.

Optional Reading (about 8 minutes)

  1. Epictetus, Discourses 2.18.1–7 (3 minutes) This is the passage we'll read aloud. How does repeating an activity strengthen a corresponding habit, and what does that ask you to watch in your own repetitions?

  2. Epictetus, Discourses 2.10.1–4 (2 minutes) Begin with the roles of human being and citizen. What responsibilities follow from shared membership before Epictetus turns to more particular roles?

  3. Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 6.50 (1 minute) Marcus considers what remains possible when an undertaking is blocked. Which virtue might a change of plan now call for?

  4. Seneca, Letters 20.1–3 (2 minutes) Seneca asks for evidence of philosophy in how a person lives. What agreement does he seek between a person's principles and conduct?

Think About

We'll choose a response for the shared case and rehearse it. These questions follow the steps. You can use a made-up example, and nothing needs to be written down.

  • Notice. What is happening, who is affected, and which responsibilities belong to you?
  • Choose. What response could you justify? Complete “I will… because…” and name a cue that would remind you.
  • Follow through. What would carrying it out look like? What change would require adapting it, and what could you learn from a missed cue?

Key Terms

For reference during the meeting. No need to memorize these.

Appropriate action (kathēkon). An action that fits its circumstances and for which a reasonable justification can be given. Consider the people affected as well as your aim.

Roles and relationships. The responsibilities that come with being a parent, coworker, friend, or participant. They answer to the wider responsibilities of a rational, social human being.

Virtue (aretē). Excellence in reason and choice. Wisdom, justice, courage, and temperance guide what a fitting response should express.

Implementation intention. A modern planning form connecting an occasion to a response: “When this happens, I will do this.” Keep the reason available so you can adapt.

Habit. A response becomes easier to call upon through repetition. What you repeatedly do matters, including when it strengthens an unhelpful disposition.

Making Good Use of Circumstances. Finding and performing the fitting response available when a plan changes. The curriculum also calls this Infinite Opportunity.

Reserve Clause. Pursuing an undertaking with allowance for circumstances that could prevent completion.

The Fundamental Rule. Recognizing what belongs to your judgment and intention and what also depends on circumstances or others. An intended action can be interrupted.

The Discipline of Action. Training appropriate conduct, execution, and steadiness. March's question is How can I make appropriate action a steady practice?
