Guide to Starting a Stoa

Step One Build Your Foundation

  • Read through our Quick Start Guide for New Stoa Leaders to learn more about what it takes to start a stoa. This is not a list of what you must do, but some thoughts on how to get started.
  • When you're ready to launch, register your stoa. We will remain engaged with you via one-on-one emails or chats. Then after you have conducted a few meetings, you will be eligible to become a member stoa.

Step Two Find a Decent Place to Meet

Generally noisy places don't work for obvious reasons, and restaurants aren't great, either, since you'll have to chat between food orders. Some places that work for many communities include:

  • Outdoors (hiking or sitting, weather permitting)
  • Indoor public atria
  • Reserving a room in a library, university, community center, etc.
  • Quiet coffee shops, pubs, or bars without table service
  • Members' homes (not recommended for new stoas)

Step Three What to Do in Your Community

Our member stoas do a lot of different things, so there's no one fixed way to run a meeting. There are two general points that do apply to many groups, though:

  • Do what your members want! Ask if they prefer readings, practice groups, or something else.
  • It helps to have a point of focus at each meeting, whether it be a topic, a type of format that you repeat, or a reading. Open formats tend to wander and feel unfocused.

Here are some ideas for topics or formats to get you started for your first several months:

  • Stoicism 101 - for your first meeting, give a brief talk and discussion on your understanding of Stoicism, then open it up for discussion.
  • Read and discuss the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Stoicism, which is a fairly easy read and a good introduction to Stoicism.
  • Focus on readings or videos from our resources section. Don't bite off more than you and your attendees can chew, though! You can digest a book together over several months.
  • Euthydemus - Socrates does a good job in this dialogue explaining why Wisdom (close to virtue) is the highest good, which makes for an excellent introduction to Stoicism. The NYC Stoics recommend the relevant portion of the dialogue (278e–282e): topostext.org/work/93. It's a great intro to a major concept in Stoicism.
  • Massimo Pigliucci has a solid Stoicism 101 essay here or read Donald Robertson's Collection. You could also get lots of material from Massimo's essay collections here.
  • Have a "topic of the month" for discussion, where you give a brief introduction to a common problem many people may have, and talk about it from a Stoic perspective. Topics can include anger, anxiety, friendship, and more.

Step Four Advertising Your Stoa

A calendaring or event service is a great way to help people find your stoa and RSVP for meetings. Good options include:

  • Meetup - offers a free, limited version that is well suited to new or small stoas. Learn about Meetup Starter.
  • Luma and Eventbrite - both are free, with additional services available for a fee.

We don't advocate for any particular service. Each region seems to have its own preference for what works best, and there are other options we haven't listed here.

Prefer not to use an online event service?

Post paper flyers in appropriate settings, such as community bulletin boards. Download a flyer template to get started:
