Get the Word Out

Most stoas grow from a few people who saw a flyer, found our map, or heard about it from a friend. Use at least two of the channels below, and keep using them after your first meeting.

Part of Start a Stoa. Already meeting? See Run a Stoa.

On the map

Register Your Stoa

Register your stoa to appear on our map of stoas. It takes about five minutes, and you can do it before your first meeting. Once we've reviewed it, we'll:

  • Add your group to the map, where people near you are already looking.
  • Connect you with people in your area who've told us they're looking for a group. Send each of them a personal invitation to your next meeting.
  • Stay in touch through one-on-one emails or chats as you get started.

Registering puts you on the map. Becoming a member stoa comes later, once you've held three meetings and have a co-facilitator.

2

In person

Post Flyers

Flyers bring in fewer people than online listings, but the people they bring tend to stay. Post them where people go to improve themselves:

  • Libraries and community centers
  • Gyms and yoga studios
  • Bookstores and coffee shops with a community board
  • College and university bulletin boards

Put your next meeting date and your Substack link (or a QR code to it) on every flyer, and replace them each month. Download a template to get started:

  • Flyer template - A4 (PowerPoint)
3

Online

Start a Substack Page

Substack gives your stoa a free home page and mailing list. Two short posts per meeting keep regulars coming back and show newcomers what your group is like.

Set up once (about 15 minutes)

  1. Create a free publication named for your stoa, such as “[Your City] Stoa.”
  2. Write a short About page: when you meet, where, and what to expect.
  3. Recommend The Stoic Fellowship's Substack from your page, and use your page's link when you register.
  4. When you have a co-facilitator, add them as an admin.

Announce each meeting, about a week before

[Month] meeting: [Topic]

[Day, date] · [Time] · [Place]

This month's question: [from your meeting guide]

Reading: [link, about a page]

New? Just come; no preparation needed. Reply to this email if you plan to attend.

Post a short recap, within a few days

  • Where the discussion went
  • The best question someone asked
  • This month's practice, and the next meeting date

Recaps keep members who missed a meeting connected, and they show visitors what a meeting is like before they come. Substack also has an optional group chat, but members need the Substack app for it, so don't rely on it for announcements.

4

Personal

Word of Mouth

  • Ask regulars to bring a friend who might enjoy it.
  • Your Substack recaps are easy to forward. Encourage members to share them.
  • Tell people in your own life. Many stoas begin with a few friends.
5

Optional

Online Event Services

In some areas, people look for local groups on event services. If that's true where you live, a listing can bring in more visitors. Options include:

  • Meetup offers a free, limited version that is well suited to new or small stoas. Learn about Meetup Starter.
  • Luma and Eventbrite are both free, with additional services available for a fee.

We don't advocate for any particular service. Each region seems to have its own preference, and there are other options we haven't listed here. Whichever you choose, keep using Substack for your regulars.

What to expect

Early meetings of three to five people are normal. Growing to 8–12 takes months, not weeks. Keep meeting on the same day each month, and keep posting.
