Find Your Co-Facilitator

A stoa run by one person tends to end when that person's life gets busy. A second facilitator is the best insurance your group has, and having one is a milestone for becoming a member stoa. Here's how to find the right person and make it stick.

1

Why

Why Two of You

  • Cover absences. Illness, travel, and busy months happen. The meeting still goes ahead.
  • Share the prep. Announcements, recaps, and setup take half the time with two.
  • A second voice. Different questions, and less temptation to lecture.
  • Continuity. If you move or step back, the stoa carries on.
  • Membership. Three meetings and a co-facilitator make you eligible to become a member stoa.
2

Who

Who to Look For

Start watching at your first meeting. The best co-facilitator is rarely the person who knows the most Stoicism. Look for someone who:

  • Comes back, two or three meetings in a row
  • Arrives early or stays late
  • Helps without being asked: setting out chairs, welcoming a newcomer
  • Asks questions that draw others in, rather than show what they know
  • Seems to enjoy the group, not just the topic

Look for someone who complements you. If you love the readings, a partner who's good with people may be the better choice.

3

Ask

How to Ask

Ask after your third meeting, in person and in private. Be specific: people say yes to a defined role with an end date, and “maybe” to an open-ended one. For example:

“I've noticed you've been here every month, and you make newcomers feel welcome. Would you co-facilitate with me for three months? In [month] you'd co-host with me, in [month] you'd lead the reading or the practice, and in [month] you'd run the meeting while I join in. After that, we'll decide together.”

  • Give them the role card so they can think it over.
  • Ask one person directly. Asking the whole group for “a volunteer” rarely works.
  • If they say no, thank them and ask someone else next month. A no isn't a failure.
4

Trial

The Three-Month Trial

  1. Month one: co-host. They welcome people, share the ground rules, and keep time.
  2. Month two: lead a segment. They lead the reading or the practice; you lead the discussion.
  3. Month three: run the meeting. You join in as a member, and resist jumping in.

After each meeting, take ten minutes together: what went well, and what to try next time.

In The Guided Year, the trial lines up with sessions four to six, and each of those sessions shows that step.

5

Official

Make It Official

  • Tell the group, and name your co-facilitator on your Substack's About page.
  • Make them an admin of your Substack page.
  • Invite them to the monthly Facilitator Roundtable.
  • Let us know their name so we can add them to your stoa's record, and when you're ready, apply to become a member stoa.
  • Renew every six months. A quick check-in: keep going, swap roles, or step back. Stepping back gracefully is far better than drifting away.

The Role Card

Print it, fill in the bottom together, and hand it to the person you're asking. It turns “would you help?” into a clear, short commitment.
